# Leading vs lagging metrics (reference)

## When

- Choosing or explaining **metrics** for SIT or SST (discovery, conversion, service scheduling).
- You need a **shared vocabulary** for predictive vs outcome metrics.

## Outcomes

- Teams align on which metrics are **leading** (signal early, actionable now) vs **lagging** (confirm results, outcomes).

## Definitions

- **Leading** — You can influence it in the near term; it predicts future results (e.g. step completion rates, friction signals, efficiency ratios).
- **Lagging** — “How much happened” — volumes and end states after the fact (e.g. total visits, appointments booked).

### SIT (illustrative examples)

- **Lagging (outcomes):** Total visits, VLP/VDP visits, share of traffic, overall referral volumes.
- **Leading (drivers):** Ratios and efficiency signals (e.g. SIT engagement vs site traffic), referral conversion rates, error or friction counts, traffic source / entry patterns that explain movement in lagging metrics.

### SST (illustrative examples)

- **Lagging:** Users starting SST, funnel completion by step, appointment booked rate, end-to-end completion rate, drop-off summaries.
- **Leading:** Per-step completion rates, time on step, dealer selection behaviors, services selection signals, reach/confirm rates before booking — especially **by step** and **by brand** (Toyota vs Lexus) when reporting.

**Rule of thumb:** Leading = change it today and expect a future result to move. Lagging = measure what already happened.
